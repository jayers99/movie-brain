# Dashboard contract — read model, chips, drawer, trailer window, find-my-row (moved from CLAUDE.md 2026-09-21)

- `_VIEW_SQL` drives from `films` with a LEFT JOIN on the criterion listing (not an inner join) so Mode-B films with no Criterion listing are visible; `list_views` keeps Criterion parity by filtering to no-listing OR current OR rated (unrated departed still hidden). `FilmView.url` is nullable (populated only from a Criterion listing; Mode-B/discovery films are always `None` here) and `FilmView.criterion` is a bool. `FilmView.metacritic_url` is set at match/promotion time and is still populated and served on the API, but since the 2026-09-07 drawer redesign nothing on the client reads it — the drawer's links row is Criterion, TMDB and CheapCharts only; only Python touches the field now — the repository, the model and both test suites. `summary()` stays criterion-scoped plus a `discovery` count — it is the MAINTENANCE view (`movie-brain status`, `/api/summary`); the dashboard header no longer mirrors it. Since 2026-09-07 the header shows four catalogue-wide counts computed in `app.js` from the films payload: films, reachable (the default scope's own predicate, so the number and the toggle cannot disagree), owned, rated by me. The dashboard's old three-way scope toggle is GONE (2026-09-07 chip redesign): its job moved into the Reachable chip, and the default view is every film. **Reachable** means somewhere to watch it today — a current Criterion listing, ANY current listing on a streaming service (subscribed or not) or the Apple store, OR owned (owned is watchable, and ownership on Apple is proof of a store presence TMDB's patchy US data missed: 18 of 28 owned films read unreachable on 2026-09-07 for that reason), OR holding an `itunes` external id (a CheapCharts product page exists only for a title the Apple store sells, and TMDB's provider feed lags new digital releases by weeks — the rule's original proof case, The Odyssey 2026, turned out on the 2026-09-21 audit to be ANOTHER film's product (a Marcel Walz film of the same title, since removed from the film) — the lag is real, that example was not; 23 of 163 unreachable films held one when measured); rated/watchlisted are judgements and do not count. "Not reachable" is the complement. The provider worklists (`films_for_provider_refresh`, `films_for_first_check`, `films_for_watchlist_refresh`) are driven by the film's TMDB id in `external_ids`, never by the legacy `tmdb.found` flag — nine resolver-keyed films the old title matcher had marked not-found sat outside the refresh for two weeks. `domain/filters.py::reachable` and `app.js`'s `reachable` are the same predicate, shared by the header count and the chip. A saved `scope=criterion` URL maps onto the Criterion chip; `scope=all` is the new default. `FilmView.services` entries are `{name, subscribed, kind, quality, has_apple_app}`, ordered by `_SERVICES_SQL` on the same four keys `domain/watch.py::rank_key` uses; the drawer splits them into "Also streaming on:" (svod) and "Buy on:" (store, which also adds a "Find on CheapCharts" search link). `export csv` writes `list_views` unfiltered, so it also includes discovery films (empty `url` cell) — this is intended, not a bug.
- Canned-filter thresholds and chip names live ONLY in `domain/filters.py`; JS reads thresholds from `/api/config`. Keep `CHIP_PREDICATES` in `app.js` and the chip buttons in `index.html` in lockstep with `_PREDICATES`. **The bar (2026-09-07 redesign) is four three-way chips and four plain ones (Rewatch joined 2026-09-19, Shop 2026-09-20), everything off by default:** Reachable (`reachable`/`unreachable`), Rated (`unrated`/`mine`), Criterion (`criterion` = on the Channel now, departed excluded / `leaving` / `criterion_new` = arrived on the Channel within `NEW_ARRIVAL_DAYS` = 30, "this month" / `not_criterion` = nothing on the Channel now, departed included), Watchlist (`watchlist`), Owned (`owned`/`not_owned`), On a list (`multi_list`), Rewatch (`rewatch` — see Old ratings below), Shop (`shop` — films worth buying, brief `docs/superpowers/briefs/2026-09-20-shop-chip/brief.md`, stories pinned in `tests/web/test_shop_chip.py` on their own server with a fresh `FakeAccount`: not owned, `my_rating` unset (0 is a rating, the 2004–08 stars are not), on no service I have, holding an `itunes` store id (`cheapcharts_url` — NOT TMDB's store listing, so the list and the drawer's Wishlist-it button cannot disagree), and not wishlisted — the owner chose that a wishlisted film is DECIDED, so it leaves the list the moment the click lands and returns on un-wishlist; "a service I have" is read from RAW fields in both mirrors — a `services` entry with `kind == 'svod'` AND `subscribed`, or `criterion and not departed` — never from `best_source`, and the `svod` check is load-bearing because the `apple-tv-store` row is itself `subscribed = 1`; 310 films on 2026-09-20, 109 with On a list), then the list picker and Clear. A cycle chip carries its keys in `data-cycle` and its labels (off first) in `data-labels`; one click cycles off → A → B → off; the keys are ordinary predicates, mutually exclusive only in the UI, so URL state is still just `chips=`. Removed keys (`top_ratings`, `recent`, `pending`, `departed`, `new_arrivals`, `needs_revisit`, `suspect`, `acquire`) are unknown now and dropped from old URLs. `multi_list` (labelled "On a list"; threshold `MIN_LISTS` = 1 since 2026-08-30 — the KEY keeps its old name because it is encoded in URL state) counts `f.lists.length`, not weighted trust, so downgrading a list's trust never drops a film off the shortlist; while it is active the default sort leads with `canon_score` desc (the sort the retired `acquire` chip carried).
- The `acquire` chip ("Canon, not owned") was RETIRED in the 2026-09-07 bar redesign: On a list ✓ plus Not owned reproduces its filter minus the Metacritic ≥ 90 clause, and its canon-score sort moved onto On a list. `canon_score` — `Σ trust × (1 − (printed_rank − 1) / size)`, no membership floor (design D12) — stays defined canonically in `domain/filters.py` and mirrored in `app.js`; `is_canon` stays. `acquisition_candidate` is gone.
- `needs_revisit` is user-response data on the watchlist pattern: own table (film_id, marked_on, optional note), the dashboard drawer toggle / its API is the only UI writer, sync never touches it; its chip was retired in the 2026-09-07 bar redesign (`review revisits` lists the flagged films). It is cleared automatically when the film's review is resolved, when `repair years --apply` fixes the year, when the film is merged away (loser), or when it is tombstoned. `review revisits` prints the flagged worklist.

- **Copy the title** (owner request 2026-09-22, pinned by `test_drawer_copy_button_sits_before_the_star_and_copies_the_title`): `button.copy-title` sits in the drawer's `h2` between the title text and the watchlist star, carries the title in `data-title` and writes the TITLE ALONE (no year) to the clipboard through `navigator.clipboard.writeText`; it reports success itself (⧉ becomes ✓ for 1.2 s) because the toast is the dashboard's error voice, and a refused clipboard toasts `Could not copy the title`. - **Trailer link** (brief `docs/superpowers/briefs/2026-09-20-trailer-link/brief.md`, backlog 4's trailer half, migration 028; seven stories pinned under their own names in `tests/web/test_trailer_link.py` on their own server): the drawer's links row now STARTS with `▶ Trailer` (`button.trailer-link`, a button dressed as a link so there is no `href` to reach the URL; owner chose "first" on the mock-up because it is then in the same spot on every film) or, for a film with no stored trailer — looked up and empty, or never looked up — a grey `a.trailer-search` "Find a trailer on YouTube ↗" that opens a YouTube SEARCH in a new tab. **A search result is never played inside movie-brain**: CheapCharts' "bogus trailers" were measured to be TMDB's first five videos with the `type` label ignored (clips, featurettes, a critic's review), and `domain/trailers.py::pick_youtube` is the whole fix — site YouTube, Trailer before Teaser, English or no-language before the film's OWN language and never a third (a third language is a dub), `official` first, then the larger `size`, at most `MAX_YOUTUBE` = 3, keys validated against YouTube's alphabet because they go into a player URL. Measured 2026-09-20 on 400 random live films (`scripts/discovery/trailer_spike.py`): TMDB 61% overall / 89% of films holding a store id, Apple's preview 92% of store films, 99% of store films together, 243 of 244 picks alive and embeddable; 30 of 30 picks read by title were trailers. **Trailers are looked up AHEAD of time, never when the drawer opens**: `film_trailer` (film_id PK, `tmdb_id` / `itunes_id` = the ids the lookup was made UNDER, `trailers` = JSON play order of `{source: youtube|apple, ref, name}`, `[]` = looked up and nothing found, `fetched_on`) is written ONLY by `enrich trailers` through `write_trailers`, sits in `_ONE_ROW_TABLES` (merge survivor-wins), and the worklist's staleness test uses `IS NOT` so a film with no store id compares equal to a row looked up with none; `TrailerTarget.itunes_ids` carries EVERY store product the film holds (a removed product has no preview, its re-listing may) while the row is stamped under `MIN(value)`. `application/trailers.py::enrich_trailers` is `enrich_credits`'s shape: one iTunes lookup per 150 films then one paced TMDB call per film (`TmdbClient.movie_videos`, the body's `original_language` and the videos in ONE answer; a foreign film whose English list holds no Trailer-TYPE video — a teaser does not count — is asked once more in its own language and picked over both); a TMDB 404 is "no videos" and still stamped (the film keeps Apple's preview), any other TMDB failure leaves the film for the next run, and Apple being down aborts BEFORE its batch is stamped. `infrastructure/itunes.py::ItunesLookup` believes a preview URL only when it is `https` on an `.apple.com` host (it becomes a `<video src>`); it is the LOOKUP endpoint by ids already held, not backlog 23's title search. `/api/films/<id>` carries detail-only `trailers`; `/api/films` does not grow; `summary()` gained `trailers`. **The window** (`#trailer`, top level in `index.html` beside `#toast`, `position:fixed; inset:0; z-index:20`, needs its own `#trailer[hidden]{display:none}` because it is `display:flex`): over the WHOLE browser window, autoplaying with sound (the click is the permission; verified in real Chrome at its default autoplay policy), never in the URL or history; caption `Title (year) — name · YouTube` / `— Apple's store preview` via `textContent` (TMDB names are community text); a `YouTube · Apple` switch when the film holds both and neither has failed; Apple plays in a narrower frame (`#trailer-frame.apple`, the previews are 640 px wide). YouTube's IFrame API script is injected on the FIRST trailer, never at page load (the dashboard must not need YouTube to boot; the tests route that URL to a stub recording every player in `window.__yt`), host `youtube-nocookie.com` as the top-level `YT.Player` option; never add `no-referrer` — YouTube refuses an embed that sends no Referer (error 153, which is also why the mock-up must be served over http). Fall-through: `onError`, the script failing, no READY within `window.MB.trailer.readyMs` (8 s; the clock starts with the SOURCE because `iframe_api` is only a loader for a second script; an advert plays after READY so it cannot trip it) or the `<video>`'s `error` marks the source failed and plays the first source that has NOT failed — so Apple failing after a hand-made switch goes back to a working YouTube — and no API at all skips every YouTube source at once; past the last, `This trailer won't play here.` plus the search link. Every callback is guarded by a per-play token and the fall-through is deferred (`setTimeout 0`), because YouTube calls `onError` from inside the player it would destroy. Keys: ONE capture-phase `keydown` listener — while a trailer is up Esc closes IT (`stopImmediatePropagation`; the drawer's own Esc handler closes through `history.back()`, which lands later, so a test must WAIT before asserting the drawer stayed) and ↑ ↓ are swallowed; otherwise plain `T` (no modifier, not in an input) plays the trailer. T and the link act only when `state.openFilm === drawnFilm` — right after ↓ the white row has moved but the new film's details have not arrived — and any drawer redraw (`openDrawer`) and `hideDrawer` close the trailer, so Back never orphans it. The hint reads `↑ ↓ previous / next film · T trailer` and `#drawer h2`'s right padding grew to 250 px for it. Known limits: a player that reaches READY and then shows YouTube's own "unavailable" pane without an error does not fall through (the switch is the way out); once the owner clicks inside the YouTube player the keyboard belongs to YouTube; a film added by sync shows the search link until `enrich trailers --apply` is run again; a pick is only as good as TMDB's filing.
- **Find my row** (brief `docs/superpowers/briefs/2026-09-20-find-my-row/brief.md`, six stories pinned under their own names in `tests/web/test_find_my_row.py`, which runs on its OWN 80-film server because the shared 10-film seed cannot evict a row from the virtual-scrolled DOM): while the drawer is open the open film's row is lifted above the dim so it stays white (`tr.lit.edge` — `position:relative; z-index:5` on the **td**s, between `#drawer-backdrop` 3 and `#drawer` 6, `#toast` 7; `pointer-events:none`, so a click on it falls through to the backdrop and closes the drawer); after the close the last film opened keeps a grey `tr.marked` row with a dark left bar. Both are drawn by `rowClass(f, i)` from state on every `renderRows`, so the virtual scroll cannot lose them; `state.mark` is memory only — never in the URL, gone on reload, untouched by Clear, and it rides with its film through filters and sorts. A row is `lit` only when `id === state.openFilm` AND the drawer is showing (`readUrl` sets `openFilm` before the drawer exists) AND the row sits wholly below the sticky header (`i * ROW_H >= wrap.scrollTop` — lifted any higher it would paint over the header). `hideDrawer` is the one close choke point and redraws rows. **↑ ↓ with the drawer open step through `state.filtered`** (`stepDrawer`): the white row moves at once, `drawerSeq` lets only the last film's details draw, `revealRow` nudges the list arithmetically (the target row may not be in the DOM), the drawer scrolls to its top, and a failed fetch puts the row, mark and URL back on `drawnFilm`. `openDrawer(id, mode)` takes `'push'` (a click), `'keep'` (boot / popstate) or `'step'` — a step REPLACES the film in the current history entry and leaves `drawerOpenPushed` alone, so one Back / ✕ / Esc closes the drawer however many films were stepped through; never reuse `'keep'` for a step, it clears the flag and the next close pushes a stale `film=` entry. Plain arrows belong to stepping while the drawer is open (they no longer scroll the drawer's content); arrows with a modifier, or typed in an input / textarea / select, are left alone; they do nothing at the ends of the list. **When the open film has LEFT the shown list** (wishlisted under Shop, rated under Unrated, un-starred under Watchlist — every chip) the arrows carry on from the gap: `openIndex` remembers the open film's index, re-found BY ID in `trackOpenIndex` on every `applyFilters` (`updateFilmLocal` replaces film objects) and kept, clamped, while the film is out; ↓ opens the film now at that index, ↑ the one before; nothing is white meanwhile, closing leaves no grey mark (the mark rides with its film), and a film that was never in the list (`openIndex` null — a pasted link) leaves the arrows quiet. **Move on** (brief `docs/superpowers/briefs/2026-09-24-move-on/brief.md`, nine stories pinned under their names in `tests/web/test_move_on.py` on its OWN 80-film server with a fresh `FakeAccount`): since 2026-09-24 the gap is only ever left by a chip, the search box, the list picker, a column filter or Back — when a drawer EDIT (a rating, the ★, the ♡: the three edits a filter reads) is what dropped the open film, `moveOnIfLeft` takes the step at once, to `min(openIndex, filtered.length − 1)` through `moveDrawerTo` ('step' history mode, so one Back still closes). It is called on the three edit paths only, never inside `applyFilters`; a film that was never in the list stays put; an empty list moves nothing and the drawer stays open. The moved-to drawer carries one grey line under its title (`.moved-on`: "Wishlisted Pan's Labyrinth · Undo") remembered in `movedOn` — only the last edit, drawn by `openDrawer` for the line's own film and cleared by any other draw and by `hideDrawer`. Undo performs the exact reverse call (DELETE wishlist / PUT the previous rating / POST the watchlist toggle); the row comes back at once and the drawer goes back to the film only if nothing was stepped, clicked or closed since Undo was pressed (the handler compares the `movedOn` object it captured). A failed wishlist Undo keeps the words and shows `Couldn't reach CheapCharts.` with `Try again` in Undo's place; a failed rating or star Undo toasts and keeps its Undo. Shop stories 3 and 4 were amended for this (Shop brief 1.1). **One click switches films** (brief amendment 1.1, owner request after first use): the backdrop's click handler asks `dimmedRowAt(x, y)` — the TOPMOST element under the backdrop via `elementsFromPoint`, so a row scrolled beneath the sticky header or the `pointer-events:none` white row both read as "no row" — and calls `moveDrawerTo(i)`, the same path a step takes (`'step'` history mode, so one Back still closes); any other click on the dim closes as before; under the dim a title link or rating box is just the row; the backdrop's cursor is `pointer` over a row only. The `tbody` click handler itself still ignores clicks while the drawer is open (they can only be keyboard-made). The hint `span.step-hint` lives in `index.html` beside `#drawer-close` (since the trailer link it reads `↑ ↓ previous / next film · T trailer`), and `#drawer h2` carries right padding so a long title cannot run under it.
