A chip for films worth buying — stories and mock-up, round 1

You asked for one button that leaves only the films you might wishlist or buy: not owned, not rated, not on a service you have, and for sale on Apple. Below are six stories on your real catalogue (a copy from this morning) and one mock-up with two variants. The variants differ in one thing only: what happens to a film that is already on your wishlist. Press Walk it on a story and the mock-up sets itself up.

The stories you keep become the automated tests, word for word. The chip needs a name; three are offered beside the variants, and everything on this page follows the one you press.

What may surprise you

  • "For sale on Apple" means movie-brain holds the film's Apple store id — the same thing the ♡ Wishlist it button needs, so every film in the list has the button. Two films that TMDB says Apple sells, and the twenty or so from backlog 23 that the store lookup misses (Under the Skin, Pineapple Express…), are not in the list until that lookup is fixed.
  • "A service I have" means the 14 you marked as yours, free ones included: Criterion Channel, Apple TV+, HBO Max, Peacock, Prime Video, Kanopy, Plex, Tubi, MGM Plus and the rest. A film on Tubi is not a candidate. A film on Netflix, Hulu or Disney+ is — Schindler's List is in the list.
  • Rated means any rating today, 0 included. Your 2004–08 stars do not count as a rating: two old 1★ films are in the list, wearing their red badge.
  • There is no "not interested". A film you look at and pass on stays a candidate, and the grey "last opened" mark is forgotten on reload. With 310 films that may get old; a pass button would be the natural next step. Not built here.
  • Trailers: movie-brain has no trailer link. The drawer's Buy on: Apple TV Store link opens the film in the Apple TV app, where I believe the trailer sits. Unchanged by this feature.
  • Only in variant B: a film you wishlist leaves the list at once, behind the open drawer. For that to feel right, ↑ ↓ must carry on from where the film was — today they go quiet when the open film leaves the list (I named that at the last delivery). B changes that, for every chip.
  • The wishlist click here is pretend — one second, nothing sent. The real one takes 5–10 seconds and talks to CheapCharts.

Real in this mock-up: all 343 films the chip finds, plus 225 others for contrast (your catalogue's top films and the ones the stories name) — titles, scores, lists, owned, services, hearts, read from a copy of your database this morning. Left out: the other 4,439 films, the filter row, most of the drawer. Nothing here can write anywhere.
