A trailer link in the drawer — stories and mock-up, round 1

You asked for an easy way to watch the right trailer for any film: a plain link in the detail drawer, the trailer coming up centred over the whole browser window, inside movie-brain, playing at once. Below: the answer to your first question (which source), then seven stories on your real films, then one mock-up with three places for the link. Press Walk it on a story and the mock-up sets itself up. The trailers are real and they really play — this page needs the internet.

First order of business: which trailer source?

Answer: TMDB's trailer list, filtered to real trailers, played from YouTube — with Apple's own store preview as the stand-in. The modal window is not difficult; both play inside the page.

Why CheapCharts looks bogus. CheapCharts takes the first five videos TMDB files under a film of any kind — clips, featurettes, interviews, reviews — and shows them all as "trailers". TMDB labels every video with a type; CheapCharts ignores the label. Keeping only the ones TMDB calls Trailer fixes it: I read the titles of 30 picks at random and all 30 were trailers (one was a box-set trailer: Paisan got the BFI's "War Trilogy" trailer).

Every one of the 244 YouTube picks was checked against YouTube: 243 still exist and allow playing inside another page; the one dead pick (Sleeping Beauty) had a second trailer that works. Apple's previews are direct video files — nothing to go wrong, no adverts — but they are small (640 pixels wide), so YouTube goes first and Apple stands in. OMDb has no trailers. A plain YouTube search is where reviews and reactions come from; it is never used to play anything.

What may surprise you

  • About a third of your catalogue has no trailer anywhere — almost all of it films Apple does not sell: shorts, rarities, festival titles. For the films you shop for, it is 99 in 100. Story 6 asks what those films should show.
  • You will often get the restoration trailer, not the original one. When TMDB files several trailers I take an official one first, then the sharpest. For Army of Shadows that is STUDIOCANAL's 4K restoration trailer, not the 1969 one. There is no "other trailers" menu in this round.
  • "Official" is TMDB's word, and four picks in ten are not. For older films the only trailer is often on a collector's channel (HD Retro Trailers is the most common). They are still the real trailers — that is what the 30-title check was for.
  • Apple's preview is small and soft (640 pixels wide), so it shows in a smaller window. I could not watch them myself: I believe they are the store's trailers, but you are the first check that they are not just a scene from the film — stories 4 and 5 play two of them. When a film has both, the trailer window offers a small YouTube · Apple switch so you can compare.
  • Once you click inside the YouTube player, the keyboard belongs to YouTube — Esc then does nothing for movie-brain. ✕ and a click on the dark area always work. If you do not click into the player, Esc closes the trailer and a second Esc closes the drawer.
  • Sound: in Chrome the trailer starts with sound because your click counts as permission. Safari may start it muted or paused; one click on the player fixes it. I have not tested Safari.
  • YouTube may put an advert in front, and it decides what it shows when the trailer ends. Apple previews have neither.
  • T plays the trailer while the drawer is open — so browsing Shop becomes ↓, T, Esc, ↓, T, Esc. ↑ ↓ do nothing while a trailer is up. My addition; say if you would rather not have it.
  • Trailers are looked up ahead of time, not when you click — one run over the whole catalogue (about ten minutes), the way cast lists were fetched. That is how the link can be absent when there is no trailer instead of failing after the click. A film added later has no link until the next run; a trailer YouTube removes later falls through to Apple's preview, as in story 7.
  • Foreign films: trailers in English or in the film's own language only — never a dubbed trailer in a third language (TMDB offers an Italian-dubbed one for Rosencrantz and Guildenstern Are Dead; it is skipped).

Real in this mock-up: ten of your films read from a copy of your database today — titles, scores, lists, owned, services, overviews — and every trailer, fetched from TMDB and Apple today.
