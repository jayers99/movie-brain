A viewing log — stories and mock-up, round 1

You asked for an exhaustive record of what you watch: one film, many viewings, each with a date, a simple barometer and maybe a short note — most films watched to be entertained and deserving only the barometer, never a rank or an analysis. Ruled already: the 0–10 stays per film, edited exactly as today; the ranker, the chips, the header counts and the export are untouched. Below: the one question this page decides, then eight stories on your real films, then one mock-up in which the log really works (in the page only — nothing is written anywhere). Press Walk it on a story and the mock-up sets itself up. What you log stays logged while you move between stories, so story 7 shows what you did in stories 1–6.

First order of business: what is the barometer?

Your five steps — didn't like → liked → worth a rewatch → worth deep analysis → worth comparing with other films — mix two questions: did I like it? and is it worth more? A film you disliked can still deserve study (After Hours, story 3). So the mock-up offers three shapes, and the barometer buttons in the drawer change with your pick:

VariantThe click that logs a viewingThen, on the logged lineWhat it cannot say
A · felt + worthDidn't like or Likedany of three tags: worth a rewatch, worth deep analysis, worth comparing—
B · your ladderone of the five stepsanother step replaces itdisliked and worth study
C · A plus "Didn't finish" (my pick)Didn't like, Liked or Didn't finishthe same three tags — greyed out on a Didn't finish line—

Why C is my pick: your study notes say an abandoned viewing is data and never a verdict (a bail is a finding, not a rating). C records the bail without pretending it was a judgement. It costs one more button. Say A, B, C, or something else — "none of these" is a fine answer.

What you should know before saying yes

  • Logging touches one other thing only: a film marked Unseen loses that mark the moment a viewing is logged (stories 5 and 6). The 0–10, the ranker, the Rewatch chip, the watchlist, the wishlist and the header counts do not move. Removing a viewing puts nothing back — the Unseen button is one click away if you want it.
  • One line per day. A second click on the barometer the same day changes that day's line; it never makes a second one (story 8). A viewing on another date is another line — change the date on a line to move it (story 4).
  • Your 2004–08 rentals show in the same history, greyed, with their stars, and cannot be edited there: oldratings stays their only writer. Today's drawer shows only the LATER of two rentals; the history shows both (Switchblade Sisters, story 2).
  • The "Me, 2004–08" line the drawer shows today goes away — its rental is now a line in the history. The Rewatch chip still reads the stars the same way.
  • The Watched chip shows every film with at least one logged viewing, newest viewing first, and stacks with the other chips like any of them. The 2004–08 rentals do NOT count as viewings for the chip — they are a different signal with their own chip. Variant 2 adds a column with the date of the last viewing, sortable like the others; the column is empty for every film until you log something.
  • Nothing is imported. No Apple TV played dates (the export has none), no Letterboxd, no dates invented for the 378 films you have rated — their rating dates are rating dates, not viewing dates. The log starts empty on the day it ships.
  • Not in this build: moving the 0–10, sealed recall, the notes-versus-critics loop, a rating history, Phase B. The note field is a plain short line — no dictation, no formatting.
  • A failed save (the server refuses, the laptop is asleep) shows the red toast the rating box already uses — Could not log the viewing — and the line does not appear; nothing half-saved.
