What you should know before saying yes

  • One verb writes the log: movie-brain viewings add, run by me from your dictation. It takes the title (or a film id once you have confirmed one), an optional date (today unless you say otherwise), an optional service from the registry, an optional rating, and your words on standard input. It writes one viewing (film, date, service) and one note holding your words whole, sets the 0–10 if you said a number, and clears an Unseen mark. It refuses and writes nothing when the title is ambiguous, unknown, the date is in the future, the service slug is not in the registry, or the rating is not a whole number 0–10 — and it says why, on one line.
  • Which film: the verb itself runs your ladder, not me. It reads which film the dashboard has open in its drawer (the page tells the server when the drawer opens and closes); a title that matches the open film wins; otherwise exactly one catalogue film with that title (apostrophes, accents and case do not matter — Pandora’s Box is found by "Pandora's Box"); otherwise it lists the candidates and stops, and I ask you. A title the catalogue lacks is a stop too: I propose films add ttNNN, it shows you TMDB's title and year, and only your yes creates the film.
  • One viewing per film per day; many notes. A second dictation about the same film the same day adds a second note to the same viewing (and moves the rating if you gave a new number). The next day the same words are a new viewing; "about last night" puts them on last night's line (--on).
  • The rating is only ever the number you say. No number, no write — I Am Cuba stays a 9 after "still astonishing" (story 5). A range ("a 7 or 8") is a question back to you, never a guess. "Put it in tier 2" is not written by the log at all: tiers and ranks stay the ranker's, through the drawer's own Tier row and Rank this (story 6).
  • The drawer only reads. Its Watched block lists every viewing newest first — date · service · the first words of your note, click to read all of it — with your 2004–08 rentals below them, greyed, as today (the "Me, 2004–08" line becomes a line in this list). No buttons: a wrong entry is removed by telling me (viewings remove, story 7). Nothing puts an Unseen mark back; the Unseen button is right there. The watchlist star, the wishlist heart, the ranker's placements, the Rewatch chip and the header counts do not move; only the "rated by me" count moves, when a rating is new.
  • The Watched chip shows every film with at least one logged viewing, newest viewing first, and stacks with the other chips; the Watched column (after My rating) shows each film's last viewing date and sorts like the others, never-logged films last either way. Rentals do not count as viewings for either. Before anything is logged the chip shows one line: No viewing logged yet.
  • Remarks about the copy (a restoration, a stretched transfer) stay inside your note and I ALSO append a row to observations/presentation.tsv, the hand-kept presentation log, exactly as it is kept today. No new code reads or writes that file.
  • Nothing is imported. The log starts empty. Sans Soleil's 2026-09-21 viewing goes in by dictation (story 2), and so would the six dated Apple TV plays, one sentence each; no dates are invented for your 394 rated films — their rating dates are rating dates.
  • Not in this build: the drawer as a place to log (no buttons, no date box), a tier or rank from the log, tier calibration (showing a tier's top and bottom film when placing — a named follow-up), documentaries as a separate pool, any import, a watch plan, essays or media as further artefact kinds (the table is built for them; only note ships), and a notes-versus-critics loop.
  • Failure: a refused command writes nothing, not half a viewing. If the dashboard is not running the drawer signal is simply absent and the ladder starts at rung two. If a stale drawer signal is left behind (you closed the browser with a film open) it can only ever pick among films of the SAME title, so it cannot log the wrong work. A reload of the dashboard shows what the database holds; the page keeps nothing.
  • Story 4's new film is drawn from what TMDB says at creation (title, year, director); its scores, overview and poster arrive with the enrichment tail that runs inside films add --apply, so the mock-up leaves them as dashes rather than invent them.
