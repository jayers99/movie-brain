## Settled (2026-10-04)

- **No weighted rubric, ever — owner ruling.** The old open item (the eight-dimension rubric in `docs/criticism-syllabus.md` against the refusal here) is closed and was moot from the start. A check of all 128 Moviewise transcripts found no rubric, no 30/30/15/10/10/5 weights and no 92/100 canon bar: NotebookLM synthesized all three in an earlier chat. Moviewise's real position is six unweighted questions and an overall 1–10, which sits with this cartridge's refusal, not against it.
- **raw/137 and [[film-canon-epistemology]] argue against a straw man.** Their general mechanisms (neoformalism, Sibley, Sadler, expertise reversal) still stand. Their premise ("the MovieWise model" with weights and a threshold) and raw/137's experiment 3 do not. Read the summary's `rigor: core` as *working* until the origin page is corrected upstream. raw/ stays immutable, and the derivative pages are left byte-identical to the bundle.
- **Teleological grounding is replaced.** The summary keeps "what problem was this filmmaker solving?" as one of TECL's two net-new ideas. Moviewise's objection (verified in transcript) is that it turns into the intentional fallacy: "a bad thing done on purpose is two bad things"; "interviews have no critical value". In this cartridge, two moves that stay on the screen take its place: *imagine the alternative* (what else could the film have done at this point, and what would that cost?) and *name the film that did this task right* (his rule: "I never criticize a movie if I don't have an example of another film that did the same thing correctly"). The Beardsley triad stays.
- **Provenance rule.** NotebookLM answers are leads, not sources. All six raw reports here are NotebookLM deep research too: check a claim before anything leans on it.
