# The Film Study Loop — Sealed Recall and the Delta

The run-loop of a film-criticism tutor. The goal is not to watch films; it is to sharpen the learner's ability to read, argue, and carry a film's craft home, so that each viewing deposits something durable. The loop is deliberately small; do not grow it before ten films have run through it.

## The engine: seal the recall before any criticism

This is the one non-negotiable step. The learner records a free recall of the film before reading or hearing a single word of criticism about it: five minutes, spoken or written, unpolished. First image that stuck, first moment the body reacted, one thing not understood. **The tutor never summarises first**, never offers a reading first, and never primes the recall with what the critics say.

The product of the whole loop is the delta between the learner's reading and an attributed critic's. A delta needs an uncontaminated pass: expose the learner to a critic first and the delta is gone for that film, along with any way of knowing what the learner's own eye would have found. Contamination is irreversible, so the seal is a hard gate.

The seal also runs per scene during the debate ([[film-socratic-debate]]): the learner's read of a scene enters the record before the corpus opens on it.

## The pre-watch brief: fifteen minutes, no more

1. No synopsis. No encyclopaedia entry, no liner-notes essay. All of it is saved for after; a preconception spends the recall before it is earned.
2. One paragraph of context only: who made the film, when, and why it is positioned where it is. Then stop.
3. Pick one eye. One craft channel from the [[cinema-flavor-wheel]] to track for the whole film (light, staging depth, sound, blocking), plus one fallback. Pick by contrast with the previous film, or pick the thinnest channel so far; record the assigned eye and the eye actually used, and let the ledger decide between the two rules.
4. Set the room. Phone elsewhere, subtitles on, no pausing, one sitting.
5. Pre-commit to the catch: the five-minute recall, ready the moment the credits roll.

The permission line, for a foreign or non-linear film: feeling behind for the first ten minutes is the correct experience, not a failure of attention.

On a rewatch, add one step: before pressing play, the learner says what is remembered, and each item is later scored held, drifted, or imported from another film. Pick the eye against the trace, not with it.

## The four buckets

After the recall is sealed, the tutor gathers criticism from the attributed-critic corpus and sorts every point into four buckets against the recall.

| Bucket | Meaning | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Convergent | Learner and critic both saw it | Calibration — the eye works |
| Blind | Critic saw it, learner missed it | The curriculum |
| Original | Learner saw it, critics never mention it | Keep. Do not discard. |
| Contested | Learner and critic disagree | Learner writes one sentence defending the read |

Track only the Blind bucket over time, tagged by craft channel; after ten films the tags cluster, and the cluster names what to study next. **The syllabus is written by the misses**, not designed in advance; the curriculum produces the criteria, the criteria do not gate the curriculum ([[film-canon-epistemology]]).

The standing warning: a beginner treats every divergence from a professional as an error, and that instinct destroys the thing worth protecting. The same eye in the same hour can produce a genuine Blind and an Original-grade observation; the buckets exist to tell the two apart.

## The delta engine

The delta only means something if the other side of it has a name. Every bucketed point is attributed to a specific critic, so the learner is calibrated against a position that can be quoted and disagreed with — never a fused consensus. The tutor retrieves and attributes; it does not author verdicts, and it tags every scene-level claim sourced or inferred, because the tutor has claims about the film and the learner has the film. The buckets are the scoreboard; the debate is the training ([[film-socratic-debate]], [[can-taste-be-tutored]]).

## Guards on the input

Presentation quality is a gate, not a preference: a film watched in a defective presentation (a stretched transfer, a substitute score) is re-queued with the fault named, never rated. Source first, verdict second.

Abandonment is data, and no justification is owed. Three kinds of bail are three findings: access (the channel, not the film — re-queue with the fix named), aversion (taste data — name the aversion), and state (wrong mood — re-queue, no note). Only a completed viewing can produce a rank or a canon verdict. A second bail at the same point is replication, which upgrades it to a preference datum.

## Timing between films

- Vocabulary is learned between films and deployed after them, never during a viewing ([[cinema-flavor-wheel]]). A document in the lap converts watching into scanning-for-instances.
- An immediate rewatch is a perception move, not a memory move: two sequences, not the film, while plot-tracking load is near zero.
- A full rewatch goes three or four films downstream, not the same week.
- Interleave by contrast, never by similarity. Three films by one director in a row is blocked practice — it feels productive and teaches least (the Kornell and Bjork painting study: styles were learned better with the artists shuffled than blocked).
- A short film before the feature is a legitimate warm-up: it switches the learner from naming mode into seeing mode.
