# Taste — What the Tutor Targets

A tutor that aims at taste needs a definition it can measure against. This page fixes one by elenchus and maps it onto tutor moves, under [[film-canon-epistemology]]'s division: taste is the response, critique is the defence, the canon is only the pool.

## The elenchus

| Proposed | Killed by |
|---|---|
| Taste is what you like | Liking is a state — it moves with mood and company. And you can like what you judge bad (the guilty pleasure), so liking and taste come apart |
| Taste is what you prefer | A preference is one ranking; taste is the *disposition that generates* rankings across cases. One film is a preference; the pattern is taste |
| Taste is subjective, so it cannot be argued | Then "I have good taste" is meaningless, and it is not. Kant's point: a judgment of taste is felt privately yet claims that others *should* agree — which is exactly what makes it arguable |

## What survives: three parts, three tests

Taste is (1) a **stable disposition** of response across cases, (2) that **discriminates** — instances can be told apart (Hume's delicacy, the trainable part), (3) and that the learner is **willing to defend**. Bourdieu adds that much of it arrives socially, which is why sealed recall exists. One test per part:

- *Stable* — does the disposition hold across cases, and across the learner's own creative work, not only across films watched?
- *Discriminating* — can the learner sort unfamiliar frames? ([[film-study-protocol]]'s first success test, in the terms of [[film-craft-taxonomy]] and [[cinema-flavor-wheel]])
- *Defended* — can the learner name what the preferred thing does that the alternative cannot, and the cost accepted?

## Arguing for a taste is showing the trade

Never assert the liking; show the trade. Worked example: planimetric staging buys stability, legibility, and a figure that carries; it costs depth, discovery, and risks the diorama. Recessive staging buys a travelling eye and hierarchy by scale; it costs a visible apparatus that composes for you. Preferring planimetric is a taste only once that trade is stated and its cost owned.

## Three tensions a taste tutor must hold

1. **Production versus a consumption-shaped canon programme.** A 150-film canon is consumption-shaped. Resolution: convert more, not consume less. Sealed recall, the ranking, and the defended argument are production acts; the friction of making a thing and watching it fall short is the ghost-score delta. Make every viewing a production act; never reduce viewing.
2. **Stability versus evolution.** A taste that never changes is arguably no taste at all. Both hold if the tutor measures stability of the *disposition* (the trade the learner names), not of the *rankings*: a ranking that moves with a new defended reason is growth; one that moves with mood is noise.
3. **Measurement without collapse into consensus.** Grading taste by agreement with critics makes the canon inherit one critic's taste. Gradable instead: discrimination (sorting tasks), the defence (evidence, consistency, the named cost), calibration as a *separate* skill, and the say-no discipline (rejecting the fine to protect the great).

## Hume's five marks as tutor moves

| Mark of the true judge | Tutor move |
|---|---|
| Delicacy | Discrimination drills |
| Practice | Reps |
| Comparison | Heats |
| Freedom from prejudice | Sealed recall |
| Good sense | The defended argument |

## Design principle: taste-gated production over consumption

When generation is cheap, judgment becomes the bottleneck, so the tutor's polarity reads *taste-gated production over consumption*, held honest by two clauses. The gate is rejection, not selection: reviewing a hundred generated options is consumption in disguise; the measure is the quality of the defended no, never volume. And hand production stays where the friction is the lesson: where the motor act builds the eye, automated production removes exactly what teaches. Compressed: the material is generated, the hand makes the rep, taste makes the cut. Components with one right answer get the expectation-misconception loop; those with relative standards (the reading, the ranking) get heats and defended preference, evidence offered and no verdict.

Two generic claims close the page. Taste is built by producing committed judgments that can fail (predict before reveal, a defended ranking, a heat verdict); making the artifact is one route, not the only one. And exposure explains a disposition's origin, not its validity: inherited liking is told from taste by a disconfirming set, blind ranking within a corpus, the reasons test, and the evolution test.
